# Persona Review R2 — Sunita, 45 · Hindi-first, low English (NEW)

Branch `feat/overnight-build`, code-traced. Week simulated: onboarding → first workout → logging meals ("2 roti, dal") → errors and everyday screens. Assumption: she reads Hindi (Devanagari) comfortably, English slowly and partially; numbers and icons are her main interface.

## First impression
The app is **100% English, everywhere, with no language option at any level.** No i18n library, no locale setting, no translated string in the codebase (grep for i18n/locale/translation across `src/` finds only `en_US` in `src/app/layout.tsx` metadata and date formatting). The splash/onboarding flow (`src/components/onboarding/*`) is text-first: every decision — goals, experience, schedule, body details — is an English sentence she must parse. She likely stalls on screen 1.

## What worked
- **Units are right for India by default:** onboarding unit state defaults to `'kg'` (`src/components/Onboarding.tsx` L50), height in cm with an ft/in toggle (`PersonalDetailsScreen.tsx`). No lbs conversion burden unless she opts in.
- **Numbers do a lot of the talking:** calorie ring, big tabular numerals, macro cards with `/ target` (`MealsPage.tsx` ~L1990-2060), steppers (+/−) in the voice logger and barcode portion picker. Where the UI is numeric, she can operate it.
- **The Hindi voice *workout* logger is a real, thoughtful feature.** `src/lib/voice-logger.ts`: Devanagari digit mapping (०-९, ~L59), Hindi word replacements (~L68-137), romanized Hinglish numbers ("assi pe paanch" = 80 × 5), `createSpeechRecognizer` for `en-IN`/`hi-IN` (~L346). `VoiceWorkoutLoggerModal.tsx` has a visible language toggle pill (Hinglish / हिन्दी, ~L309-325), example chips including "assi pe paanch", big +/− steppers, a 4-second auto-apply countdown, and plain result cards. If she ever reaches an active workout, logging a set by speaking Hindi is genuinely possible.
- **One jargon island is explained well:** the RPE picker (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx` ~L3531-3610) pairs every number with words — 6 "Warm-up / Light — Could do 4+ more", 8 "Sweet Spot", 10 "Maximum Effort". DOTS gets a "What is the DOTS Score?" explainer (`ProgressPage.tsx` ~L1718).

## Friction & bugs
- **SUNITA-1 (blocker): zero localization.** No Hindi UI, no language picker in onboarding or Settings, no Hinglish fallback copy. Every other finding below compounds this: she cannot switch the app into the one language she reads. This alone ends the week on day 1 for a Hindi-first user.
- **SUNITA-2 (major): onboarding jargon density is untranslated *and* unexplained.** Goal subtitles (`FitnessGoalsScreen.tsx`): "Hypertrophy volume & progressive overload", "Calorie deficit & lean mass preservation", "Work capacity, stamina & cardiovascular fitness", "Longevity, joint health". Experience screen (`ExperienceLevelScreen.tsx`): "structured periodization", "progressive overload", "work capacity". These are hard for fluent English *beginners*; the icons (dumbbell/flame/shield) help her guess "exercise / burn / strong" but cannot distinguish "Lose Fat" from "Get Stronger" reliably — and picking wrong changes her calorie prescription (see Meera review: the goal silently sets her target).
- **SUNITA-3 (major): workout-screen jargon is mostly unexplained.** RPE is explained in its picker (credit above) but appears bare everywhere else — "Avg @8 RPE", "RPE drift" (`WorkoutPage.tsx` ~L2208-2283), coach copy "Central nervous system exertion is elevated… neuromuscular exhaustion" (`ProgressChart.tsx` ~L177). "e1RM / 1RM / tonnage / PR" appear across Progress and coach surfaces with **no explainer found in code** (grep for plain-language definitions returns only the DOTS one). The deterministic coach was written to a "plain English, no unexplained jargon" rule (`coach-engine.ts` header) — plain *English* is the operative word; it is still English, and still uses RPE inline.
- **SUNITA-4 (major): she cannot log her actual meal in her actual language.** The meal logger is text-only (no microphone for food — voice is workout-sets only). The offline parser (`parseNaturalMealOffline`, `src/lib/macros.ts` ~L1050) splits on commas/`and` and matches English `FOOD_DB` keys: "2 roti, 1 bowl dal" works ✅, but "do roti aur ek katori dal" — "aur" is not a separator, food keys are English, her input is rejected as junk ("Couldn't recognize… Try a food name like…", in English, `MealsPage.tsx` `rejectJunk`). Devanagari script input ("२ रोटी") has no path at all: the Hindi handling in `voice-logger.ts` covers spoken gym numbers, not food text. The AI parse route *might* understand Hinglish (UNVERIFIED — depends on the LLM, nothing in `src/app/api/ai/parse-meal/route.ts`'s prompt mentions Hindi/Hinglish), but its errors and confirmations are also English-only.
- **SUNITA-5 (minor): error states are calm but English and jargon-tipped.** `ErrorState.tsx`: headline "Something went wrong" is as plain as English gets, and "your records remain safely stored" is reassuring — but actions read "Return to Dashboard", "Clear temporary cache & force restart", "Technical Diagnostics". Toast errors elsewhere ("Unlikely Quantity", "Not Recognized") are short English imperatives she may not parse; none are bilingual.
- **SUNITA-6 (minor): icons never carry meaning alone.** In code, critical actions always pair icon + English text (goal cards, scanner tiles, nav). That's good design for sighted English users and a dead end for her: there is no icon-only mode, no pictorial exercise-first browsing in onboarding, and exercise names in plans are English text lists (`Onboarding.tsx` plan-offer ~L655-675). Whether the icons alone are guessable is **UNVERIFIED** (not testable from code) — but the app never gives her the chance to rely on them.
- Note (not a bug): age/weight/height forms use labelled numeric inputs with unit chips — the most Sunita-friendly screens in the app, precisely because they're numbers with minimal prose. Even there, validation errors are full English sentences ("Please enter a realistic bodyweight between 30 kg and 300 kg").

## Keep-or-delete verdict
**DELETE — she cannot use this app today.** Not a quality judgment on the training engine: a Hindi-first, low-English 45-year-old cannot complete onboarding with confidence, cannot log "do roti aur dal", and cannot read the errors when something goes wrong. The single bridge that exists — the Hinglish voice set-logger — sits three English walls deep inside a workout she has to configure first. Until there's a Hindi UI (even partial: onboarding + meals + errors), recommending ASCEND to Sunita is recommending an app in a language she doesn't read.

## Competitor gap
HealthifyMe ships Hindi UI and Hindi/Hinglish food logging aimed exactly at this demographic; Google Fit/YouTube-based routines need no reading at all. ASCEND's Indian *food database* is deeper than both for home cooking — and completely locked behind English input parsing, which is the frustrating part: the asset exists, the access path doesn't.

## Rating: 2/10

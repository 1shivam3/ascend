# Vikram Persona Review — ASCEND (`feat/overnight-build`)

**Reviewer lens:** Vikram, 35, time-poor professional. Trains exactly 3 days/week (Mon/Wed/Fri), 45 min max per session, usually 6 AM. Will abandon anything slow, modal-heavy, or tap-hungry. Read-only analysis of the codebase; every defect below was verified against the code and cites file + line.

---

## 1. First impression (30 seconds in)

He opens the app at 6 AM, half-awake, and the first thing he hits is a full-screen splash photo with an 1.8-second auto-advance (`src/components/onboarding/SplashScreen.tsx` — auto-advance timer at ~line 20) and a "Tap to continue" gate. Then a welcome screen with a big Get Started button. Not terrible — but onboarding then marches him through **five screens before he ever sees a workout**: Goals (multi-select + Continue) → Experience (single tap, auto-advances — actually fast) → Personal Details (name/age/weight/height/gender form) → Plan Offer ("Want your training plan?") → accept/decline. That's ~2 minutes on the guest path, fine for his 3-minute budget. But if he taps "Get Started" instead of the tiny underlined "Continue as Guest" link, he lands in **account creation (email/password/Google OAuth)** first (`src/components/Onboarding.tsx`, `WelcomeScreen.tsx` — `onGetStarted` → `create_account`). A time-poor man will not verify email at 6 AM; he picks Guest or he leaves.

The real gut-punch lands one screen earlier, though: **onboarding never asks the one thing that defines his life — how many days he can train.** He completes the whole flow and ASCEND hands him a 4-day Upper/Lower split (Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri). He trains Mon/Wed/Fri. The app is wrong about his week from the moment he says yes. See section 3.

## 2. What worked for him specifically

- **The 45-minute fit is accidentally right.** `Onboarding.tsx:306` hardcodes `preferredDurationMin: 45` into every training profile, and the pre-built Full Body / Upper-Lower plans run 5 exercises (~17 sets), which is a sane 45-minute program for an experienced lifter who keeps rest tight. The number happens to match his life; it just wasn't asked.
- **No guilt machinery for the missed Friday.** The coach engine's own tone rules say "plain English, no unexplained jargon, no shame or guilt language" (`src/lib/coach-engine.ts:16`), and the missed-session check-in copy is explicitly "warm, never guilt-tripping — life happens, and the goal is the next session, not the missed one" (`src/lib/coach-engine.ts:1027-1029`). The streak system silently auto-consumes a freeze for one missed day with "no modal or fanfare" (`src/lib/streak-freeze.ts:17`). On a work-trip Friday, Vikram wakes up Saturday to no shaming. That's exactly right for him.
- **Auto-rest-timer starts without asking.** In both workout modes the timer auto-starts when a set is logged (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:1061-1088` advanced, `:1204-1266` beginner) with sensible adaptive durations — 180s for heavy compounds ≤6 reps, 90s for compounds, 60s for accessories (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:651-662`). He doesn't have to configure anything; the one thing the app assumes (he rests between sets) is correct for his heavy days.
- **Quick Log actually works and is genuinely one-tap-per-plan.** `LogPastWorkoutModal.tsx:146-183` (`handleQuickLogPlan`) converts a whole planned workout into a completed session with prescribed weights/reps, sets `plannedTotalSets` honestly, and saves in one button tap after selecting the plan. Two taps + done. The missed-Friday scenario is the best-implemented flow in the app for him.
- **No fabricated PRs / honest finish.** `handleConfirmFinish` (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:1541-1681`) only PRs from completed sets, never fabricates RPE, and records `plannedTotalSets` from the full session so an interrupted session can't report "100%". If he bails at minute 42, the record says what it says. A time-poor man who needs the truth, not a gamified lie, will trust this.
- **The wake lock is on** during a session (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx`, screen wake-lock effect) — his phone won't sleep mid-set at 6 AM.

## 3. What confused or frustrated him (specific, cite screens/flows, count taps)

### BUG 1 — Onboarding never asks days/week; he's locked into a 4-day plan (the killer)
`src/components/Onboarding.tsx:52` declares `const [daysPerWeek, setDaysPerWeek] = useState<number>(4)`. **Nothing ever calls `setDaysPerWeek`.** The days/week selector does not exist — I grepped every onboarding screen; `PersonalDetailsScreen` collects name, age, weight, height, gender only. So every user — including Vikram — gets `daysPerWeek: 4` → `buildDefaultWeeklySchedule(generatedSplit, 4)` → Upper/Lower on Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri (`Onboarding.tsx:305-320`, `workout-schedule.ts:544-554`). He trains Mon/Wed/Fri. Consequences, all real:
- **Wednesday: the app shows him a Rest Day.** `HomePage` hero renders "Recovery is training too" copy when `getTodaySessionState` returns the rest-day title (`HomePage.tsx:404-412`). He opens the app at 6 AM on a training day and is told to recover.
- Tuesday/Thursday the app expects him in the gym while he's at work; his "This week X/Y workouts" strip (`HomePage.tsx:320-351`) counts against the wrong denominator, so his consistency metric is garbage from day one.
- Fixing it requires: Train tab → scroll past plans and built-in routines to the "Current Split" card → "Adjust plan" → `WeeklyScheduleModal` → regenerate with correct days (`WeeklyScheduleModal.tsx:58` even derives the default from `trainingProfile?.daysPerWeek || 4`, so the modal itself suggests 4 days again). Minimum 6–8 taps, buried three levels deep, for the single most important fact about his life.
- Bonus insult: the code to honor 3 days already exists (`workout-schedule.ts:556-567` maps 3 days to Mon/Wed/Fri Full Body, and `Onboarding.tsx:289-291` routes `daysPerWeek <= 3` to the full-body split). The engine supports him; the onboarding just never asks. And note the secondary bug: if he somehow did get 3 days, the full-body branch uses hardcoded template loads (e.g. Squat 92.5 kg, Deadlift 110 kg in `workout-schedule.ts:356-390`) while only the 4-day branch runs the bodyweight-scaled `getGoalAdaptiveSplitTemplates` (`Onboarding.tsx:283-300`) — so the 3-day path ignores his bodyweight and experience entirely.

### BUG 2 — "Start Workout" from Home takes 2 taps to reach iron (and can show 3 taps)
`HomePage.tsx:235-257`: `handleStartWorkoutFromHome` saves a workout draft and calls `onNavigate('workout')`. But `WorkoutPage` only renders `ActiveWorkoutScreen` when its local `isModalOpen` is true (`WorkoutPage.tsx:2636`); arriving with a draft shows an "IN-PROGRESS WORKOUT DETECTED" banner (`WorkoutPage.tsx:2019-2055`) that demands a second tap on "Resume" (`onClick={handleResumeWorkout}`, `WorkoutPage.tsx:2045`). Tap count at 6 AM, half-awake: **Home hero "Start Workout" (1) → Resume banner (2) → in workout**. The in-app "Resume" is pure ceremony — the draft was created 200ms earlier by the previous tap. Strong opens the session on tap one; Fitbod too. This is a half-second-of-loading away from being right, and it isn't.

### BUG 3 — Default workout mode makes him dismiss a full-screen celebration after EVERY set
`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:2161` gates the fast flow on `userMode === 'beginner'`: if Vikram picked "Intermediate" he gets the advanced multi-set table (`handleToggleSetComplete`, one checkbox tap per set — fine). But the store **defaults to `'beginner'`** (`src/lib/store.ts:462`), and any user who picks "Beginner" (or is never shown the distinction again) gets this per-set sequence in `handleCompleteActiveSet` (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:1204-1266`): tap "Complete Set" → auto-rest-timer starts → **full-screen "Set Complete!" overlay opens (`completedSetSummary`, rendered at `ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:3532-3619`)** → he must tap "Next Set" (`handleProceedNextSet`, `:1268-1281`) to get back to lifting. That's **2 taps per set minimum**, plus an interstitial takeover every ~60 seconds. For his ~17-set session that's 34 taps and 17 full-screen takeovers. Then, per exercise, the effort prompt ("How hard was that exercise today?" — `showEffortPrompt`, `:2221-2315`) demands one more rating tap + a "Next: …" tap before moving on: **2 extra taps × 5 exercises = 10 more taps**. Session total ≈ **44 taps just to navigate logging**, before a single weight adjustment. A man who supersets two exercises would be in modal hell: the overlay has no "jump to other exercise" — his only escape is the X dismiss per set. There is **no superset support anywhere in the codebase** (zero grep hits) — for a 45-minute lifter who supersets to save time, this is a structural miss, not a nit.

### BUG 4 — The compress-to-45-minutes feature exists in the engine but has no button anywhere
`handleCompressWorkout(mins)` in `WorkoutPage.tsx:900-905` calls `compressWorkout(exercises, mins)` — exactly what Vikram needs when he has 30 or 45 minutes instead of an hour. It is **never called from any JSX** (grep: only the definition and import). Dead code. Meanwhile `HomePage` tells him today's session is "~40 min" via the `exerciseCount * 8` heuristic (`HomePage.tsx:313-318`) — but the auto rest timers it will actually enforce (120–180s on compounds, `ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:651-662`) plus 17 sets put Upper A at 55–70 real minutes. The app both promises 40 and can't offer the one feature that would make it true.

### BUG 5 — Quick Log is buried; Home has a dead "Log Past Session" wiring
Vikram misses Friday (work trip). Saturday morning, Day 7, he wants Quick Log. On the Home screen there's a `LogPastWorkoutModal` mounted (`HomePage.tsx:873-875`) — but `isLogPastModalOpen` is **never set to true anywhere in `HomePage.tsx`** (grep: the setter appears only in the onClose). The modal is dead UI on the screen he lives on. The real path: bottom tab "Train" (1) → scroll past his plans, past the built-in routines, past the rest-timer section to the "Current Split" card (scroll) → tiny "Log Past Session" text link (`WorkoutPage.tsx:2287-2294`) (2) → select plan from dropdown (3) → "Log as Complete" (4) → (optionally set Friday's date — the modal defaults to today, Saturday, with Today/Yesterday shortcuts `LogPastWorkoutModal.tsx:548-560`; a Friday trip needs the manual date picker). 4–6 interactions for the thing that should be on the Home hero's secondary row. The engine work is excellent; the discoverability is a fail.

### Friction 6 — Onboarding asks for things he doesn't need between meetings
Name, age, exact bodyweight, height (cm or ft/in toggle), gender, diet preference default — the full calibration. For a man who just wants "3 days, 45 min, heavy" between meetings, the personal-details form (`PersonalDetailsScreen.tsx`, ~380 lines of validation) is the longest screen in the flow. Equipment is hardcoded to `full_gym` (`Onboarding.tsx:307`) — correct for him by luck, but anyone training in a hotel gym on a work trip gets the wrong assumptions. Diet preference state exists (`Onboarding.tsx:53`) but is **never surfaced as a question** either — dead state alongside `setDaysPerWeek`.

### Friction 7 — First-time tips overlay ambushes the first session
`showFirstTimeTips` (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:124-134`) shows a tips overlay on the very first workout (dismissed once via localStorage). He opened the app to lift in 45 minutes; the app wants to teach first. One tap to dismiss, and the back-button handler intercepts it (`isDiscardConfirmOpen` at priority 120 vs tips not handled — actually the tips overlay just needs the dismiss tap), but it's still a screen between him and set one on the morning that matters most.

### Friction 8 — Weight/reps editing fights speed in the "fast" mode
In advanced mode (the mode he should be in), each set row requires: tap weight field → keyboard → type → tap reps field → keyboard → type → tap checkbox. Prescriptions are pre-filled (`createWorkoutExercisesFromPlan`), which helps, but there's no "same as last set, just check me off" one-tap gesture and no "repeat last session" one-tap start from Home (repeat-last exists only in WorkoutPage, `handleRepeatLastWorkout`, and only if he hunts for it). For a lifter doing the same weights as Wednesday, logging should be nearly zero taps; it's ~4 taps per set.

## 4. Would he keep using it after week 1? Why/why not

**Probably not — and the reason is BUG 1, not the polish.** By end of week 1 he has: done a wrong-plan Monday (Upper A — fine by accident), opened the app Wednesday to "Recovery is training too" (insulting on a training day), and either hacked his schedule in a modal he shouldn't need or concluded the app doesn't understand his life. He might tolerate the two-tap start (BUG 2) and even the set-complete overlay (BUG 3) if the plan were right, because the no-guilt coach (section 2) and honest logging are genuinely good. Quick Log working well is a real save on the missed Friday — that's the one moment he'd feel the app respected his reality. But the core promise — "tell me what to do today" — is wrong 1–2 days out of every 3-day week. A 45-minute man deletes apps that waste the first 2 minutes figuring out what day it is. If, and only if, the days/week question gets asked at onboarding, he stays: the training content itself (Full Body A/B/C, calibrated loads, adaptive rest) is solid for his goals.

## 5. What would make him delete it

1. **One more Wednesday "rest day" morning.** The schedule mismatch is the delete trigger — it makes the app feel adversarial, not absent-minded.
2. **The per-set celebration overlay in week 2.** 44 navigation taps per session × 3 sessions = he'll switch to the Notes app before the second Monday. The overlay was designed for a beginner's first week; for anyone past it, it's friction wearing a party hat.
3. **A session that runs 65 minutes because the timers wouldn't shut up.** Auto-rest at 180s on every heavy set (`ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:651-662`), no reachable compress button, no "I'm short today" option — he'll miss his 6:45 hard stop, be late to work, and blame the app.
4. **The in-progress banner gaslighting.** If he ever force-quits mid-session (phone call, work), the "IN-PROGRESS WORKOUT DETECTED" banner resurrects on every Train visit until he Resumes-or-Discards. Fine once; annoying forever if drafts accumulate. (Mitigated: the draft only saves after a set is completed — `ActiveWorkoutScreen.tsx:~470` — so phantom drafts from template-browsing are avoided. Credit where due.)

## 6. What competitors do better for time-poor lifters

- **Fitbod** asks days/week and session length *during* onboarding (the two questions ASCEND skips), then builds the split around them. Its workout screen is one continuous checklist — one checkbox tap per set, zero interstitials — and it has a visible "short on time" trim. ASCEND has the trim (`compressWorkout`) but hides it and has no checklist equivalent in beginner mode.
- **Strong** is the minimalism bar: open → start empty workout → 1 tap per set (check), per-exercise rest timer that can be ignored, and the history/plates UI assumes you know what you're doing. ASCEND's advanced mode approximates this (one-tap `handleToggleSetComplete`, floating dismissible timer), but Strong gets there in **one tap from the home screen**, not two via a resume banner.
- **Hevy / Strong both** let you superset natively (A1/A2 grouping with shared rest). ASCEND has no superset concept — for the 45-minute lifter, supersets are the highest-ROI time-saver there is, and ASCEND can't represent them.
- **Fitbod's** "recovery" model adapts the *next* session when you miss a day; ASCEND's coach check-in (`generateCheckIn`) is kind but passive — it doesn't propose "Friday's Lower B moved to Saturday, 30-min compressed version ready." For Vikram, adaptation beats empathy.
- **MacroFactor-style** nutrition is overkill for him and ASCEND is right not to push it — but ASCEND's Home screen gives Fuel equal visual weight to the workout hero. For Vikram, meals are noise; the home screen should be workout-first. (Minor, but it tells him who the app thinks he is.)

## 7. Rating out of 10

**5 / 10.**

The engine is a 8: honest logging, no-fabrication PRs, silent streak freezes, no-guilt coaching, genuinely good Quick Log, adaptive rest timers. The product wrapper is a 3 for this user: it never asks his two defining questions (3 days, 45 min), shows him a rest day on a training day, makes him dismiss a celebration after every set, and ships the time-trimming feature with no button. Vikram doesn't need features — he needs the app to know his week and get out of his way. Right now it knows neither.

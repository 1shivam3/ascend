# Aditya, 26 — Bodybuilder (6-day PPL + arms, 20+ sets/muscle/week, 5 years training)

## 1. First impression (30 seconds in)

Clean, premium-looking, asks my goal — I tap "Build Muscle," which is correctly described as "Hypertrophy volume, exercise selection, progressive overload" (`src/lib/types.ts:51-58`). Good start. Then the experience screen tells me *"Tap one — it sets your starting volume"* (`src/components/onboarding/ExperienceLevelScreen.tsx:75`). I tap Advanced, expecting the app to ask the only two things that define my training: **how many days a week, and how much volume.** It asks neither. There is no days-per-week question anywhere in onboarding — and that's not a missing screen, it's a bug (see §3). Thirty seconds in I've been promised hypertrophy volume and handed a generic plan sight-unseen. My spreadsheet is already looking smug.

## 2. What worked for him specifically

- **The exercise library is genuinely decent.** 104 exercises (`src/lib/exercise-library.ts`): Chest 19, Back 21, Legs 17, Shoulders 13, Arms 16, Core 11. Real isolation work exists — pec deck, both cable crossover directions, incline dumbbell curl, preacher curl, concentration curl, overhead cable extension, cable/machine lateral raises, rear-delt machine, seated *and* lying leg curl, hack squat. This is not a squat-bench-deadlift-only toy library. Custom exercises can also be added by name (`src/components/ExerciseSelectorModal.tsx:48`).
- **Hypertrophy progression is a real model, not vibes.** For `build_muscle`, the engine uses double progression — hit the top of the 8–12 range on all sets, then add load (`src/lib/workout-engine.ts:670-705`), with sensible stall handling (variation / 10% reset, `src/lib/workout-engine.ts:1005-1021`). That is how a bodybuilder actually progresses accessories.
- **Logging a 25-set session does not choke.** There is no cap on sets or exercises in the session/plan builder — `handleAddSet` and `handleAddExercise` (`src/components/WorkoutPage.tsx:1092`, `:1026`) just keep appending. My volume is at least *loggable*.
- **A real 6-day PPL exists in the codebase** — Push/Pull/Legs ×2 with a proper Mon–Sat schedule (`src/lib/workout-schedule.ts:160-225`), plus Arnold and bro-split generators, reachable later via Suggested Workouts / the schedule modal. The iron is there; onboarding just never hands it to me (see §3).
- **Progress photos exist and the upload itself works**: pick a photo, it's downscaled and saved, grid view with dates (`src/components/ProgressPage.tsx:230-260`, `:1290-1362`). Body measurements and bodyweight trends exist too — within limits detailed below.

## 3. What confused or frustrated him (specific, cite screens/flows)

**Day 1 — Onboarding never asks my training days, so my 6-day split is unreachable (verified bug).**
`daysPerWeek` is initialised to `4` in `src/components/Onboarding.tsx:52` and its setter `setDaysPerWeek` is **never called anywhere in `src/`** — I grepped; the declaration is the only occurrence. So the branches that would give me PPL for 6 days or a bro split for 5 (`src/components/Onboarding.tsx:283-291`) are dead code for every fresh user. Aditya — 6 days, PPL + arms — is silently onboarded as a 4-day Upper/Lower user. His session length is also never asked: `preferredDurationMin: 45` is hardcoded into his training profile (`src/components/Onboarding.tsx:~307`). He trains 90–120 minutes.

**Day 1 — The "build muscle" plan he actually gets is embarrassingly low-volume.**
The default `build_muscle` templates (`buildGoalAdaptiveSplitTemplates`, `src/lib/workout-engine.ts`, default branch) are Upper A 17 sets, Lower A 14, Upper B 17, Lower B 12 = **60 sets/week total**. Per muscle, per week: **chest 8 direct sets** (Bench 4 + Incline Bench 4), **arms 6 direct sets** (one curl, one tricep extension), **lateral delts 4 sets**, **calves 4 sets**, zero chest fly work, zero dedicated arm day. Aditya does 20+ sets per muscle per week; this plan gives his arms roughly a *third of one of his arm days, spread over a week*. Even if he later finds the hidden PPL, it's 16 sets/session (`src/lib/workout-schedule.ts:170-225`): chest improves to 14 direct sets/week, but biceps stay at 6 direct, triceps 6 direct, calves 6 — and "PPL + arms," his actual split, exists in no generator at all.

**Day 1 — "Advanced" changes nothing.** The experience screen promises it "sets your starting volume," but the only scaling that exists adjusts *weights* by bodyweight, with a 0.6 first-exposure factor for beginners (`scaleTemplateWeightForAthlete`, `src/lib/workout-engine.ts:~1600-1625`). Set counts are identical for a day-one beginner and a 5-year lifter — and on the (unreachable) 6-day path, experience isn't even passed to the generator (`src/components/Onboarding.tsx:285` passes only the unit).

**Days 2–7 — Building his real session is tap-by-tap drudgery.** Every exercise added to a session starts with exactly **one** set (`src/components/WorkoutPage.tsx:1026-1049`), so a 7-exercise, 25-set push day is ~25 individual "Add Set" taps plus per-set weight/rep entry, every session, unless he starts from a plan — and plan exercises are likewise built one set at a time. There are no set types beyond a warmup flag (`isWarmup`, `src/lib/types.ts:171`): **no supersets, no drop sets, no rest-pause/myo-reps** — the pump techniques his programming is built on cannot be logged as what they are. One accidental tap makes it worse: "Make Today's Workout Shorter" silently clamps every exercise to a maximum of 3 sets and deletes accessories (`compressWorkout`, `src/lib/workout-engine.ts:1251-1265`).

**Days 2–7 — There is no volume-per-muscle tracking. Anywhere.** I searched the whole `src/` tree: no sets-per-muscle, no weekly volume by muscle group, nothing. Progress (`src/components/ProgressPage.tsx`) has exactly three lenses: **Strength** (e1RM charts, PRs, and a DOTS powerlifting score — `calculateDOTS`, `src/components/ProgressPage.tsx:697-703`, modal at `:1661`), **Body**, and **Consistency** (streaks/heatmap). The Weekly Recap reports only *total* tonnage and *total* sets (`src/components/WeeklyRecapModal.tsx:38-76`). Aditya's single most-checked number — "did chest get its 20 sets this week, are arms lagging?" — cannot be answered by this app. Worse, the data model couldn't support it properly anyway: muscles are six coarse buckets (`Chest | Back | Legs | Shoulders | Arms | Core`, `src/lib/exercise-library.ts:1-2`) — biceps and triceps are both just "Arms," quads/hams/calves are all "Legs," side and rear delts vanish into "Shoulders." And any custom exercise the keyword matcher doesn't recognise is silently classified as **Chest** (`getExerciseMuscle` fallback, `src/lib/exercise-library.ts:262,280`), which would poison even a future volume chart.

**Days 2–7 — The coach speaks powerlifting, not hypertrophy.** The post-workout take is built on estimated 1RM, PRs and RPE (`src/lib/coach-engine.ts:~690-710`); its one "volume" concept is total tonnage vs. your average (`volumeLandmark`, `src/lib/coach-engine.ts:707`). There is no concept of volume landmarks (MEV/MAV/MRV), no weekly-sets-per-muscle feedback, no pump, soreness or mind-muscle-connection input anywhere in the coach. Its pattern detector will nag you about skipped *legs* specifically (`detectPatterns`, `src/lib/coach-engine.ts:~985`) but has nothing to say about a lagging muscle group — the one conversation a bodybuilder wants from a coach.

**Day 5 — Progress photos: works, then quietly betrays him.** The flow saves the photo — but: (a) the `ProgressPhoto` type supports a pose label ("Front", "Side", "Back", `src/lib/types.ts:557-558`) and the upload handler **never sets it** (`src/components/ProgressPage.tsx:247-251`), so his front/side/back shots land in one undifferentiated grid; (b) there is **no compare / before-and-after view** — the entire point of progress photos; (c) photos are capped at 24, newest-first, so photo #25 silently evicts his oldest shot — his starting physique (`MAX_PROGRESS_PHOTOS`, `src/lib/store.ts:340`, slice at `:1084`); (d) photos live only in localStorage, deliberately excluded from sync/backup, and if the localStorage write fails on quota, the failure is swallowed (`src/lib/store.ts:343-349`) while the UI still toasts *"Progress photo saved locally"* (`src/components/ProgressPage.tsx:252`). He finds out his check-in photos are gone when they're gone.

**Day 7 — Measurements: the bodybuilder's key metric doesn't exist.** Body metrics support exactly chest, waist and hips (`chestIn / waistIn / hipsIn`, `src/lib/types.ts:125-127`; same three inputs in `src/components/BodyMetricsModal.tsx`). **No arm measurement.** No thigh, no calf, no neck, no shoulders. The first number Aditya reaches for with a tape — arm circumference — cannot be logged in an app whose goal he selected is literally "Build Muscle." (Bonus insult for non-kg users: applying a schedule preset in `WeeklyScheduleModal` generates plans hardcoded to `'kg'` regardless of his unit — `src/components/WeeklyScheduleModal.tsx:132`.)

## 4. Would he keep using it after week 1? Why/why not

No — not as his training app. He'd keep it installed for a week out of curiosity about the coach's post-workout takes and the meal logging, but his training would move back to his spreadsheet by day 8. The deciding factor: the app's weekly review cannot answer his weekly question. His Sunday ritual is *sets per muscle vs. target, arm/chest measurements, photo comparison*. ASCEND gives him a DOTS score, a streak, and total tonnage. Everything he actually reviews, he'd still be maintaining by hand — at which point the app is a slower set-logger bolted onto the spreadsheet he still needs.

## 5. What would make him delete it or stick with his spreadsheet

Delete triggers, in order: (1) discovering photo #25 silently deleted his Day 1 physique shot, or that a "saved" photo never persisted; (2) the first time "make workout shorter" or a regenerated plan wipes the volume he painstakingly built tap-by-tap; (3) realising "Advanced / build muscle" personalisation is cosmetic — same sets for everyone, forever. What would make him *stay*: a per-muscle weekly set counter against targets he sets (e.g. chest 20, arms 24), an arm/thigh/calf measurement set with trend charts, photo compare by pose, and a one-tap "duplicate last session / add 4 sets" flow. None of these are exotic; all are absent.

## 6. What competitors do better for bodybuilders

- **RP Hypertrophy App** is built on exactly Aditya's mental model: per-muscle volume landmarks (MEV/MAV/MRV), mesocycle-by-mesocycle set progression, and per-exercise feedback (pump, soreness, performance) that auto-adjusts next week's volume. ASCEND has no volume landmarks and no subjective pump/soreness input at all.
- **Hevy** shows volume and set counts broken down by muscle group, full per-exercise history at a glance, supersets, custom exercises *with an assigned muscle group*, and one-tap routine duplication — i.e., the weekly bodybuilding dashboard ASCEND lacks, plus faster repeat-session logging.
- **Strong** supports warmup/drop/failure set types, per-exercise volume graphs, and body measurements that include arms, thighs and calves — the tape-measure set ASCEND truncates at chest/waist/hips.
- Even a **spreadsheet** beats ASCEND for him today: it has a column for arm size, a pivot for sets per muscle, and it never silently caps his photos at 24.

## 7. Rating out of 10

**4 / 10.** The bones are respectable — a 104-exercise library with real isolation movements, genuine double-progression for hypertrophy, and uncapped set logging. But for a bodybuilder the product misses its own brief at every checkpoint: onboarding can't even ask his training days (dead `daysPerWeek` setter) and hands a 5-year, 6-day lifter a 60-set/week Upper/Lower with 6 arm sets; "Advanced" doesn't change volume; there is no sets-per-muscle tracking, no arm measurement, no photo comparison, no superset/drop-set logging, and the coach grades him like a powerlifter (e1RM, PRs, DOTS). It is a decent general strength app wearing a "Build Muscle" label. Aditya keeps the spreadsheet.

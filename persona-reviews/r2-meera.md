# Persona Review R2 — Meera, 29 · Type-2 diabetic · Doctor-ordered fat loss (NEW, SAFETY-CRITICAL)

Branch `feat/overnight-build`, code-traced. Week simulated: onboarding under doctor's orders → prescribed deficit → daily logging → manual target edit → coach suggestions → AI coach.

## ⚠️ Headline
The *default* prescription is conservative and, for Meera's likely stats, safe. But the app **never asks about her diabetes, never warns her at the point of prescription, lets her (or anyone) type an 800 kcal target that it then praises her for hitting, runs five mutually inconsistent "safety floors", and cannot show her sugar at all.** For a medical user, the guardrails exist as a legal page she will never see, not as behaviour in the flows she actually uses.

## First impression
Onboarding asks name, gender, age, weight, height (`src/components/onboarding/PersonalDetailsScreen.tsx`) — **nothing else health-related. No medical-condition question anywhere in onboarding; `injuriesOrLimitations` is hardcoded `[]` in `src/components/Onboarding.tsx` ~L335 and never populated by any onboarding screen.** She picks "Lose Fat", gets a plan offer with no calorie number on it, and a target is silently set. Her doctor's context — diabetes, medication, glucose targets — has nowhere to exist in this app.

## What the deficit actually is (traced precisely)
- `src/lib/macros.ts` `calculateRecommendedMacroGoals`, lose_fat: `targetCalories = Math.max(1400, maintenance − 450)`, protein 2.2 g/kg. Maintenance = Mifflin-St Jeor × activity multiplier (`calculateMaintenanceCalories`).
- Meera (68 kg, 158 cm, 29 y, sedentary): BMR ≈1,362 → maintenance ≈1,634 → **target 1,400 kcal (deficit only ~234)**. Light activity: maintenance ≈1,872 → target ≈1,422. Both are moderate and defensible. Credit: there is **no** fasting, crash-diet, or aggressive-cut suggestion anywhere in the deterministic coach or the AI prompts (grepped `fast/aggressive/crash/skip meal` across `src/lib` + `src/app/api` — the only "aggressive" is coach-engine *warning against* aggressive cuts, ~L1220). The LLM guardrail in `src/app/api/ai/coach/route.ts` `baseGuardrails` explicitly says "Never recommend severe caloric restriction (<1200 kcal)".
- Coach adjustments are small (−125/+100 kcal), gated to ≥5 weigh-ins over ≥10 days, and `suggestCalorieAdjustment`'s `apply()` (`src/lib/coach-engine.ts` ~L1171) refuses to cross `SAFE_MIN_CALORIES` and instead suggests walks/tracking accuracy. The plateau answer caps trims at 100–150 kcal. This layer is well-behaved.

## Friction & bugs — safety first
- **MEERA-1 (TRUST-BREAKER): manual target edit accepts 800 kcal/day and the app praises adherence to it.** `src/components/MealsPage.tsx` `handleSaveGoals` (~L846): validation is only `cal < 800 || cal > 10000` → error. 800–1,199 kcal saves fine, with no floor warning, no gender check, no "below safe minimum" prompt. Then `goalNutritionStatus` (lose_fat branch, ~L1034) computes `isDeficitKept = eaten ≤ target` and renders **"Deficit Preserved … Maintaining caloric deficit to oxidize fat stores while preserving lean tissue"** — the app actively congratulates a diabetic user for a day at 790 kcal. Every floor elsewhere in the system (1,200/1,400/1,500) is bypassed by this one form.
- **MEERA-2 (TRUST-BREAKER): five inconsistent safety floors.** (1) Initial prescription floor: **1,400 for everyone** (`macros.ts`). (2) Coach floor: **male 1,500 / female 1,200 / other 1,400** (`coach-engine.ts` `SAFE_MIN_CALORIES` L1143-1147). (3) Meals page copy claims **"1,400 kcal safety floor"** (`MealsPage.tsx` ~L2017). (4) Manual edit floor: **800** (MEERA-1). (5) AI prompt guardrail: **1,200** (`api/ai/coach/route.ts`). A man onboarded at 1,400 is *below* his own coach's floor; a woman can be coached down to 1,200 but not prescribed below 1,400 initially; the UI text asserts a floor the edit form doesn't enforce. No single source of truth = no floor a clinician (or a user) can trust.
- **MEERA-3 (major, safety-relevant): the live coach path uses the male floor and the wrong goal for everyone.** `src/lib/use-coach-digest.ts` calls `suggestCalorieAdjustment(trend, goals[0], macroGoals)` with **no gender argument** (defaults `'male'`) and `goals[0]` instead of `resolvePrimaryGoal`. The carefully gendered `SAFE_MIN_CALORIES` table is effectively dead code in production wiring. (Direction here is conservative for women, wrong-goal is not: a multi-goal diabetic user can be advised to *add* calories under a fat-loss prescription.)
- **MEERA-4 (major): the 1,400 floor is not maintenance-relative — small/sedentary users get a surplus labelled as fat loss.** Floor is an absolute `Math.max(1400, …)` with no cap like `min(floor, maintenance − 100)`. Example: 50 kg, 150 cm, 45 y, sedentary → maintenance ≈1,262 → prescribed target **1,400 = a +138 kcal surplus** while the goal, badges and copy all say "deficit". For Meera this is the efficacy mirror-image of MEERA-1: the app can quietly prescribe *gain* to a woman whose doctor ordered loss, and nothing in the UI flags the contradiction (`calorieDelta` would show +, buried in small text on Meals).
- **MEERA-5 (major): no sugar tracking exists — anywhere.** `FoodItem` (`src/lib/types.ts` L323-332) has calories/protein/carbs/fat only; `FOOD_DB` entries have no sugar field; `ScannedProduct.per100g` (`src/lib/openfoodfacts.ts`) and `src/app/api/barcode/route.ts` drop Open Food Facts' `sugars` value even though OFF provides it. In the UI, 52 g of carbs from a gulab jamun and 52 g from dal are indistinguishable, there is no per-meal or per-day sugar total, no carb-per-meal view, and meal reminders (`src/lib/notifications.ts` ~L272) are generic "Time for breakfast. Log your meal". For a type-2 diabetic, carb totals exist (good) but the single most decision-relevant nutrient split — sugar — is unrepresentable in the data model.
- **MEERA-6 (major): disclaimers exist but never at the point of advice.** `src/lib/legal.ts` contains *only* `SUPPORT_EMAIL`, `LEGAL_LAST_UPDATED`, `LEGAL_VERSION` — no disclaimer text at all. The real disclaimer is thorough (`src/app/disclaimer/page.tsx`: PAR-Q §1, nutrition §5 explicitly "not intended to treat metabolic disorders…", plus Terms/Privacy and a Settings link, `SettingsView.tsx` ~L643-683) — but it is a standalone page. The Home calorie-suggestion card (Apply button, `HomePage.tsx` ~L545-600) carries **no** "not medical advice" note; the Meals target display carries none; onboarding shows none before prescribing a deficit. The one in-context label found is on the AI drawer: "AI Generated • Not medical advice" (`WorkoutCoachDrawer.tsx` L219). A diabetic user can use the app for a month without ever encountering a disclaimer.
- **MEERA-7 (minor): no medical tone problems in AI copy — verified clean, one caveat.** Deterministic coach copy is non-shaming and conservative (see above); the LLM system prompt (`api/ai/coach/route.ts`) forbids diagnosis/treatment and <1,200 kcal recommendations. Caveat: this is prompt-level only — there is no output validation, and the prompt receives no diabetes flag (there is nowhere to store one), so the model cannot tailor or hedge for her condition.
- **Shared with Priya (safety-adjacent):** barcode not-found confirm has no validation — `BarcodeScannerModal.tsx` `handleConfirmAddToMeal` will log a 0-kcal/0-macro item and permanently `saveCustomBarcode` it. Silent 0-kcal logging understates intake — dangerous direction of error for someone dosing decisions around food.

## What worked
- Initial prescription for typical stats is moderate (see trace above); protein target 2.2 g/kg in deficit is evidence-aligned.
- Coach never escalates a deficit aggressively; at the floor it explicitly redirects to walks and tracking accuracy.
- Disclaimer *content* (when found) is genuinely good — PAR-Q, metabolic-disorder carve-out, kidney/hydration note.
- Carb totals per meal/day do exist, which is the minimum viable diabetic signal.

## Keep-or-delete verdict
**DELETE (for her use case) — do not rely on ASCEND for the dietary side of a doctor-ordered plan.** Not because the default number is dangerous — it isn't — but because trust for a medical user means the guardrails hold in *every* path: one edit form (MEERA-1) and one mis-wired digest call (MEERA-3) are enough to break that, and she cannot even see sugar (MEERA-5). She should track food in an app with sugar fields and a medical disclaimer at the point of prescription, and use ASCEND, if at all, for workouts only. Flagging MEERA-1 and MEERA-2 as **trust-breakers** explicitly.

## Competitor gap
MyFitnessPal/HealthifyMe both expose sugar (and HealthifyMe surfaces it prominently for Indian diabetic users), ask health-context questions, and put disclaimers in the target-setting flow. ASCEND's data model can't currently represent the comparison at all.
